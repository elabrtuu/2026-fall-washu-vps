Reading Response 3

"My website is a shifting house next to a river of knowledge. What could yours be?"

1. What did you think about the essay? Did anything in particular resonate?

I enjoyed reading through the different possible metaphors that help characterize the web and the websites that make it up. Two of the ones I resonated with the most were the room metaphor and the garden metaphor, since they provided us the ability to visualize them both as websites that are complete and finite. I also appreciated the thrown rock into the deep ocean metaphor, since it allowed for a different approach to visualizing websites by emphasizing that the web can also simply be an area where we can create something beautiful and never have to worry about it again. Moreover, I liked how Prof Schwulst underlines how "the web is what we make it", since being an interconnected set of nodes is exactly the individuality it fosters and is what makes it special.

2. Laurel proposes a website could be a river, a garden a thrown rock. If you have a website, what could your website be?

I would like to think that my website would be a food platter: it's finite, and forces me to work with the space that I have to create the most cohesive and balanced dish that I can. When I visualize this metaphor, I imagine the different possible food setups representing the web elements being scattered across the screen in a variety of ways.

3. What can you with a website that you make yourself that you can’t do if someone else makes your website?

A website that you make yourself gives you the creative freedom to organize your information on the screen without having to put it into words for someone else to try to decrypt your vision. It allows for you to express yourself in a way that is unique to you in an unfiltered way, and not constrained by the limitations of someone else's vision for the website.
